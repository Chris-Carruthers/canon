WHY WRITE IT DOWN: verbal critique evaporates, and the same objection then gets
raised in three separate reviews by three people who each think it is new.

THE ONE RULE: label every point as OBSERVATION, PRINCIPLE or PREFERENCE.

  Observation — what is there. "The focus ring is 2px." Checkable.
  Principle   — a rule with a source. "Focus must be visible at 3:1 (WCAG 2.4.11)."
  Preference  — your taste. "I would rather it were filled."

All three are legitimate. Collapsing them is what makes design feedback feel
arbitrary and unarguable, and it is why designers learn to defend rather than
listen. A labelled preference can be waved off in one word by the person who owns
the decision, which is exactly what should happen to it.

CRITIQUE THE WORK AGAINST ITS OWN GOAL. Ask what it is trying to do before saying
whether it does it. Half of all bad critique is a review of a different design.
