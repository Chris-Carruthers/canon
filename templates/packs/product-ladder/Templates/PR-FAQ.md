WORKING BACKWARDS. Write the announcement first, as though the thing already
exists, in the language of the person who benefits. If that paragraph is boring,
the feature is boring, and you have found out for the price of a paragraph.

THE FAQ IS NOT MARKETING SUPPORT. It is where the hostile questions go. A PR-FAQ

has skipped the entire point of the exercise. The internal FAQ in particular must
contain the questions that could kill the idea.

# <Title>

## Press release

**Headline** — what it is, in the reader's words, in under ten.

**Sub-heading** — who it is for and the benefit, one line.

**Opening paragraph** — the announcement. Date, what is now available, to whom.

**The problem** — what was true before, and why it was bad. Written for somebody who
does not work here.

**The solution** — what they can now do. Still no implementation.

**Quote from us** — why we built it. Cut every adjective that survives deletion.

**How to get started** — the first thing they do.

**Quote from a customer** — invented, but it must be a sentence a real one would say.
If you cannot write a believable one, that is the finding.

## External FAQ

What a customer asks. Cost, availability, migration, what happens to what they have

## Internal FAQ

**The hostile questions go here**, and the section is worthless without them. What is
the hardest thing about this? What would make us cancel it? Who inside will object,
and are they right? What does it cost to run? What do we stop doing to do this?
