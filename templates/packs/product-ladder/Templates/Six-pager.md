PROSE, NOT BULLETS. This is the whole mechanic. Bullets let you omit the sentence

# <Title>

## What we are asking

The decision, in the first three sentences. Not at the end.

## Where things stand

The current state, with numbers. What we tried, what it cost, what we learned. A
narrative with no post-mortem in it reads as enthusiasm.

## What we propose

The recommendation, and the reasoning that leads to it. In prose, so the reasoning is
checkable.

## What we considered and rejected

Each alternative stated at its strongest, then rejected for a specific reason. This is
the section that decides whether the room trusts the rest.

## What this costs, and what we stop

Money, people, time, and the thing that does not happen because this does. If nothing
is being stopped, nothing has been decided.

## What could go wrong

The failure modes, how we would notice, and what we would do. Including the one that
would make us reverse this.

## Appendix

Data, tables, detail. Nothing here is load-bearing for the argument — if it is, it
belongs above.
