# Voice: Shape Up Pitch

## Write it like

**Primary — Ryan Singer, *Shape Up*, chapter 6.** Problem, Appetite, Solution, Rabbit
Holes, No-gos.

## Why them

The pitch is the only widely-published product format that treats **time as an input**.
The appetite is fixed before the solution is described — "six weeks, and if it does not
fit we cut scope, not extend" — which forces the solution to be shaped to the budget
rather than the budget estimated from the solution. Every other property of the format
follows from that inversion.

The second gift is **Rabbit Holes and No-gos as first-class sections**. Most formats
record what is in scope and leave the rest to inference. Naming the specific places the
work could sink, and the things you have decided against, is what lets a pitch survive
an engineer who has a better idea on day three — because the better idea has usually
already been considered, and the document says so.

The third is deliberate under-specification. A pitch is shaped enough to be bettable
and loose enough that the people building it still get to design. That is a hard
register to hold and it is why the format works when a spec would not.

## Do not write it like

**An estimate.** The register where you describe the solution and then work out how
long it takes.

This is the exact inversion the format exists to prevent, and it survives being told
not to, because estimating is what everybody has always done. The failure it produces
is familiar: a number nobody believes, quoted back in week five, extended twice. Fixing
the appetite first turns an unanswerable question — *how long will this take* — into an
answerable one: *what is worth six weeks*. If the appetite in your pitch was derived
from the solution, you have written a plan with a Shape Up heading on it.

The second failure is **over-specification**: pixel mockups and named components. A
precise mock gets implemented literally, and you have spent the builders' judgement on
your behalf without meaning to.

## The moves

**Write the appetite before the solution, on its own line.** Not after. This changes
what you write next, which is the whole point.

**Say explicitly that scope is cut, not time extended.** Write it in the pitch so
nobody has to relitigate it in week five.

**Sketch roughly on purpose.** Fat marker, not Figma. Precision you did not intend gets
built.

**Name the rabbit holes.** The two or three places this quietly consumes a month — an
unclear data model, an untested integration, a migration nobody has scoped.

**No-gos carry reasons.** Out-of-scope is a list; a no-go is a list plus the reason,
and the reason is what stops the same suggestion returning four times.

**Leave the middle loose.** If you are writing acceptance criteria, you have escalated
to a spec and should say so.

## Before saving

1. Is the appetite stated before the solution, as a constraint rather than an estimate?
2. Does the pitch say in words that scope gets cut rather than time extended?
3. Are there named rabbit holes — specific places this could consume a month?
4. Does every no-go carry its reason?
5. Is the middle deliberately loose, with no acceptance criteria in it?
