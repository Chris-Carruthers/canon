# Voice: Research Findings

## Write it like

**Primary — Erika Hall**, *Just Enough Research*. Findings stated at the confidence they
were actually obtained at, and no higher.

**Supporting — Steve Portigal**, *Interviewing Users*, for keeping what was said apart
from what you took from it.

## Why them

Hall's discipline is the one this document lives or dies by: **the finding is a claim,
and the claim has a confidence, and both go on the page.** Five participants is five
participants. Writing "users want X" from five conversations is not a summary, it is an
upgrade — and the upgrade is invisible to everybody downstream, who then cite it as
settled and build on it.

The other half of *Just Enough* is scope. The document should carry the conclusions and
the evidence for them, not a transcript. Research that is filed as raw material gets
read once; research that is filed as claims-with-evidence gets cited for years.

Portigal supplies the separation between observation and interpretation. "Three
participants scrolled past the filter without stopping" is an observation. "The filter
is not discoverable" is an interpretation of it. Both belong in the document; only one
of them is a fact.

## Do not write it like

**A quote-dump with a conclusion bolted on.** Twelve verbatim excerpts under a heading,
then a summary paragraph asserting something the quotes do not obviously support.

The failure is that quotes look like evidence while doing none of the work of evidence.
A reader cannot tell whether twelve quotes came from twelve people or from the same two
enthusiasts, whether the counter-examples were included, or how the excerpts were
chosen — and selection is where all the bias lives. Meanwhile the conclusion, which is
the part that will actually get cited and acted on, arrives with no confidence attached
and travels forever as settled fact.

The second failure is **the disclaimer paragraph**: a Limitations section written to be
skipped. If the sample cannot support the claim, weaken the claim rather than
apologising for it in small print.

## The moves

**One numbered finding per claim, each with evidence and a confidence rating.** High,
medium or low, with the reason for the rating.

**Put n in the frontmatter.** It then travels with every quotation of the note, which
is exactly where it is otherwise lost.

**Quotes are evidence for a stated claim, never the claim itself.** State the finding,
then the quote that supports it.

**Write what you did not learn.** The questions this did not answer and the ones it
raised. An honest gap is a finding; silence reads as coverage.

**Say what would change your mind.** The observation that would overturn the main
finding. If nothing could, it was not research.

**Record the question as it was asked before fielding**, not reverse-engineered from
what you found.

## Before saving

1. Does every finding carry its evidence and an explicit confidence rating?
2. Is n visible in the frontmatter, so it travels with any quotation?
3. Is every quote attached to a stated claim rather than standing in for one?
4. Does the note say what it did **not** learn?
5. Is there an observation that would overturn the main finding?
