# Voice: PR-FAQ

## Write it like

**Primary — the Amazon PR-FAQ** (Bryar & Carr, *Working Backwards*). A press release
written before the thing exists, followed by the questions it provokes.

**Supporting — Amazon's own launch posts**, for the register the press release half
should sit in.

## Why them

Working backwards inverts the usual failure. Most product documents describe what will
be built and infer the benefit; a PR-FAQ states the benefit as though it already
happened and forces you to make it sound like something a person would want. **If the
opening paragraph is boring, the feature is boring**, and you have found that out for
the price of a paragraph rather than a quarter.

The second mechanic is the ban on implementation. Not one sentence about how it is
built — which feels artificial and is the point. Implementation detail is the most
effective device ever invented for avoiding the question of whether anybody cares, and
removing it leaves nowhere to hide.

## Do not write it like

**Marketing.** A press release with an FAQ of comfortable questions attached.

This is the failure mode of the format, it is extremely common, and it is worse than
not writing one — because a PR-FAQ that has passed review confers a confidence that a
marketing document has not earned. **The internal FAQ is where the hostile questions
go.** What is the hardest thing about this? What would make us cancel it? Who inside
will object, and are they right? What do we stop doing to do this? An internal FAQ
without questions of that shape has been filled in rather than written, and the
exercise has produced nothing.

The tell is a document nobody argued with. A real PR-FAQ makes at least one person
uncomfortable, usually the author.

## The moves

**Write the press release first, and in the customer's words.** Not the feature name,
not the internal term. What they can now do.

**Date it, and write in the past tense.** "Available from March" — the tense is what
forces specificity.

**No implementation anywhere.** If a sentence could only be written by somebody on the
team, cut it.

**Put the killing questions in the internal FAQ**, and answer them properly. An answer
of "we will need to look into that" is honest and belongs in there.

**Write the customer quote and check whether you believe it.** If you cannot write a
sentence a real customer would say, that is the finding, and it is worth more than the
rest of the document.

**Externally: what it costs, what happens to what they have now, and how they start.**
Those three are what actually gets asked.

## Go read

- Colin Bryar & Bill Carr, *Working Backwards* — the PR-FAQ chapter
- Jeff Bezos, 2015 letter to shareholders — <https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2015-letter-to-shareholders>
- Any AWS launch announcement, for the press-release register

## Before saving

1. Would the opening paragraph interest somebody who does not work here?
2. Is there a single sentence of implementation anywhere? (There should be none.)
3. Does the internal FAQ contain at least one question that could kill the idea?
4. Is the customer quote something a real customer would plausibly say?
5. Did anybody find this uncomfortable to read — and if not, why not?
